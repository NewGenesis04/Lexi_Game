# How to Play

Like all the best games, this word game is easy to learn but difficult to master. Still, if this is your first time playing, you should familiarize yourself with the rules of the game.

Every game starts with the same set of tiles. In English, there are **100 tiles**. Common letters like the **E** appear frequently, and less common letters like the **Q** and **Z** appear only once. These less common letters are worth more points to compensate.

Valid words include words of any part of speech, except for proper nouns, abbreviations, or words requiring hyphens or apostrophes. Keep in mind that there are words that look like proper nouns but are still in the dictionary for other reasons. For example, TEXAS is a valid word — not because it's a state, but because it's the section of a steamboat that includes the crew's quarters.

---

## Basic Gameplay

To begin each game, both players are given **seven tiles** from the tile bag. The website determines which player goes first at random.

If you're going first, place a word on the board with at least one letter touching the **center star**. You may play either across or down, as long as you touch the center star. Diagonal words are not permitted. Your word must be **at least two letters** in length.

Click **Submit** or hit **Enter** to submit your word and end your turn.

Once you play your word, you will be given new tiles from the bag to replace the ones you've used. Until the very end of the game — when there aren't enough tiles left — you'll always be given seven tiles to use.

Your opponent will then take their turn. The game proceeds with players alternating turns until the end. **All letters played on a single turn must be placed in only one row or column.**

You may play your letters alongside other letters already on the board — in fact, it's encouraged! But when you do, **all** words formed by your play must be valid words according to the lexicon.

---

## Scoring

Scores are calculated automatically when you submit a word. The score of your move is the sum of the letter values in each word formed by your play, plus any points from **Premium Squares**.

There are four types of Premium Squares:

| Square | Effect |
|---|---|
| Double Letter (DL) | Doubles the value of that letter |
| Triple Letter (TL) | Triples the value of that letter |
| Double Word (DW) | Doubles the value of the full word |
| Triple Word (TW) | Triples the value of the full word |

The **center star** counts as a Double Word square.

Once a Premium Square has been used, it may not be used again — on later turns, letters played on that square score their face value only.

When forming words that connect two different Premium Squares, letter bonuses are applied first, then word bonuses. If multiple words are formed on one play using the same Premium Square, you receive the bonus for **each** word formed.

Playing all **seven tiles** in a single turn is known as a **bingo**. You receive **50 bonus points** in addition to the regular score.

---

## Blanks

Two of the 100 tiles are **blanks**. You may use them as any letter you wish, making them extremely valuable. When playing a blank, you will be shown a menu to designate which letter it becomes. The blank remains that letter for the rest of the game.

**Blanks are worth zero points** regardless of which letter you choose, but their flexibility more than makes up for it.

---

## Swapping Tiles

If you cannot find a word on your turn, you may use your turn to swap tiles instead. Select **Swap** from the menu and click the tiles you want to exchange. Those tiles return to the bag, and you receive new tiles in their place.

Swapping counts as your turn and scores **zero points**, but if you have very poor tiles — all vowels, all consonants — it can be your best option.

> **Note:** You may not swap tiles when fewer than 7 tiles remain in the bag.

---

## Challenges

You can play with **automatic word validation**, where all moves are checked against the dictionary when you attempt to submit them.

Alternatively, you can play **with challenges enabled**:

- You may make plays that are not valid dictionary words — it is up to your opponent to challenge them.
- If your opponent challenges a word and it is **invalid**, the word is removed from the board, you score zero for that turn, and your opponent gets the next move.
- If your opponent challenges a word and it is **valid**, you win the challenge. The result depends on the challenge rule in use:
  - **Point challenge** — you receive extra points for your move.
  - **Turn challenge** (double challenge) — your opponent loses their turn.

The same applies in reverse: if you challenge your opponent's word and it is invalid, it is removed and you get the next turn. If it is valid, you lose the challenge.

---

## Time

Both players receive the same total time budget for the game. This timer **does not reset** after each move — it represents the total time you have for all of your moves combined. Use your opponent's turns to plan ahead.

**Overtime:** If you run out of time, you enter overtime (shown in red):

- You immediately receive a **10-point penalty**.
- For each full minute of overtime, you lose **10 additional points**.
- Once your overtime is fully depleted, you **forfeit the game** — even if you have a higher score.

---

## Tile Bag

The tiles you have yet to see — either on the board or on your rack — are displayed on-screen at all times, showing counts for **Tile Bag**, **Vowels**, and **Consonants**.

- When the Tile Bag drops **below 7 tiles**, swapping is no longer permitted.
- When it drops to **0 tiles**, no replenishment tiles are given after plays.

When the bag is empty, the tiles displayed in the bag window are your opponent's **exact rack**. They can see yours too — use this information to guide your endgame plays.

---

## Turn History

The Turn History window shows all plays made in the current game, from most recent to least recent. Clicking any word in this window will reveal its definition.

---

## End of Game

The game ends when all tiles have been drawn and one player empties their rack. At that point:

- The player with **tiles remaining** subtracts the value of those tiles from their score.
- That same value is **added** to the score of the player who emptied their rack.

---

## Skipping and Resigning

- **Skip** — Click **Skip** and confirm to pass your turn. Generally only done when no valid move can be found.
- **Resign** — Click **Resign** and confirm to forfeit the game at any time.
